# A term charter is a better earnings anchor, but what is actually locked?

**Research date: October 12, 2026**

[中文](charter_anchor_cn) | [DHT Q4](report_en) | [FRO historical P/E](fro_2007_2009_en) | [Hub](../)

> **Conclusion:** use the company's contracted employment and remaining charter days to anchor forward earnings, rather than capitalizing a spot spike. But a charter fixes **hire terms**, not unconditional net profit. The original Fearnleys Week 41 PDF lists a **$230,000/day one-year assessment for a modern eco/scrubber VLCC on October 7, up $45,000**. This supports the scale of the user's $250,000/day discussion, but it is **not a named signed fixture**. DHT's **$100,000/day, three-year Panther charter** is a company-disclosed contract.
>
> Under the explicit cost assumptions below, **$250,000/day for 365 paid days** produces **$91.25m gross hire and $78.96m illustrative profit**. At **350 paid days**, profit is **$75.26m**. The approximately $80m intuition is arithmetically reasonable; the claim that this net profit is already guaranteed in the next calendar-year accounts is not.

All hire rates are **USD per day**, with contract duration stated separately. "$250k/one year" does not mean $250k total annual rent. Amounts marked `m` are millions of USD. This is a focused research extension, not a new company-wide valuation or a calibrated trading rule.

## 1. Static and dynamic P/E: first define the denominator

"Dynamic P/E" is not a single standardized field across data vendors. It may mean annualized year-to-date/quarterly earnings or forecast annual earnings. Always inspect the provider's definition.

| Measure | Definition used here | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Static P/E | Price / latest disclosed full-year EPS | Last year's earnings can be stale |
| TTM P/E | Price / trailing earnings | High quarters roll out slowly; publication lag matters |
| Annualized-quarter P/E | Price / (latest disclosed quarterly EPS × 4) | Seasonality and one-offs become annual assumptions |
| Forecast fiscal-year P/E | Price / explicitly estimated fiscal-year EPS | A forecast, not a reported result; remaining calendar months matter |
| Next-12-month P/E | Price / estimated earnings over the actual next 12 months | Requires the employment book, uncovered-day assumptions and all company costs |

For comparability with the previous article, keep its **October 12 reference price of $26.22**, not a claim to the latest live quote:

| DHT denominator | Approximate multiple | Evidence status |
|---|---:|---|
| FY2025 EPS $1.31 | 20.02x | Static, reported annual EPS |
| Latest four reported quarters through Q2 2026 | 8.92x | Reported-income TTM approximation |
| Latest reported Q2 2026 EPS $1.23 × 4 | 5.33x | Annualized reported quarter, not a forward forecast |
| Hypothetical Q4 profit $520m × 4 | 2.03x | Annualized scenario, not already earned |
| FY2026 scenario including that $520m Q4 | 3.68x | Earlier illustrative model, including stated one-off gains |
| Actual next-12-month, contract-supported company earnings | Unknown | Complete charter and cost reconstruction not supplied |

**20x static and 2x annualized-quarter P/E can both be arithmetically correct without proving a tenfold valuation opportunity.** The denominator changed. The earlier Q4 assumptions and their limitations remain unchanged in [the calculation note](report_en).

The dynamic column annualizes the preceding disclosed quarter, not the as-yet-unpublished current quarter. At **2008Q3-end**, annualizing the very strong Q2 made the shares look like **2.83x earnings**, even after the price peak. At **2009Q1-end**, the **1.90x** static/TTM reading coexisted with **6.59x** annualized latest-quarter earnings: recent profitability was much weaker than the old annual denominator.

However, **2007Q4's 37.50x** annualized reading preceded a strong 2008 market. The comparison diagnoses earnings composition/momentum; it does not establish that a large spread predicts a stock decline.

With a consistent share basis:

```text
TTM P/E / annualized-quarter P/E
  = annualized latest-quarter EPS / TTM EPS
```

The price cancels. This is an earnings-ratio identity, **not an independent valuation signal**. In FRO's 2008Q3 reading the ratio is **1.67**, partly supported by Q2 gains; in 2009Q1 it is **0.29**. Strip one-offs before treating it as a measure of operating momentum.

## 4. Does $250,000/day secure approximately $80m of net profit?

### The revenue arithmetic is straightforward

```text
$250,000/day × 365 paid days = $91.25m gross hire
$230,000/day × 365 paid days = $83.95m gross hire
$100,000/day × 365 paid days = $36.50m gross hire
```

A conventional time charter generally leaves ordinary voyage costs with the charterer while the owner retains technical operating expenses, depreciation and financing. Actual war-risk, off-hire, sanctions and trading clauses must be read; a time charter is not a bareboat charter.

### Illustrative owned-vessel profit bridge

These are **assumptions**, not DHT/FRO vessel-level disclosures:

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---:|
| Commission | 1.25% of gross hire |
| Operating expenses | $10,000 per calendar day |
| Calendar days with owner costs | 365 |
| Annual depreciation / assumed drydock amortization | $5.00m |
| Annual interest | $2.00m |
| Allocated G&A | $0.50m |
| Income tax | $0 assumed; not a tax conclusion |

Owner costs continue during the modeled off-hire days. Debt principal and capital expenditure are **not additional accounting expenses** in this net-profit bridge. Their cash-flow effect must be modeled separately; do not deduct maintenance capex and its amortization twice.

| Daily hire | Paid days | Gross hire | Illustrative profit after modeled charges |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $100,000 | 365 | $36.50m | $24.89m |
| $100,000 | 350 | $35.00m | $23.41m |
| $230,000 | 365 | $83.95m | $71.75m |
| $230,000 | 350 | $80.50m | $68.34m |
| $250,000 | 365 | $91.25m | $78.96m |
| $250,000 | 350 | $87.50m | $75.26m |

So **approximately $80m over a full charter year is plausible**, not absurd. Under these particular assumptions, exactly $80m would require approximately **$252,887/day** at 365 paid days. A newer/higher-cost ship, financing changes, off-hire or different commissions change the result.

**What is fixed is the contractual hire arrangement, subject to performance and terms. Net profit is not unconditional.** Counterparty default, termination disputes, unexpected repairs, uninsured costs, impairment and changes in financing can alter the outcome.

### A charter year is not necessarily the next financial year

For a **hypothetical** one-year charter running October 1, 2026-October 1, 2027, at $250,000/day and no off-hire:

| Calendar year | Hire days | Gross hire |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2026 | 92 | $23.00m |
| 2027 | 273 | $68.25m |
| Total contract | 365 | $91.25m |

The next calendar year's accounts do **not** receive the entire $91.25m revenue or approximately $80m profit from this contract alone. Earnings after expiry remain uncontracted unless another agreement exists.

At $100,000/day for three illustrative 365-day years, gross hire totals **$109.50m**, before deductions and off-hire. That is a longer stream, not $109.50m annual profit. Actual calendar contracts can include leap days.

## 5. Better candidate indicators than an isolated P/E line

These are **proposed diagnostics, not demonstrated predictive signals**.

| Indicator | Definition | Why useful / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-rate revenue-day coverage over the next 12 months | Firm fixed-rate expected paid days within the horizon and available-day population / all available fleet days in that same population | Apply off-hire exclusions consistently; variable profit share and unexercised options are not fixed cover |
| Contracted net-hire cash-cost coverage | Base contracted hire after commissions / full-fleet mandatory cash requirements over the same horizon | Can show how much expense is covered before uncertain spot revenue; requires costs for uncovered ships too |
| Expiry and renewal exposure | Quarterly expiring days plus re-fixing rate versus expiring hire, on comparable terms | Identifies the earnings cliff hidden by high trailing income; price can anticipate expiry |
| Contract-supported next-12-month P/E | Price / projected EPS from fixed net hire, explicit uncovered-day scenarios and all company accounting costs | Better matched to future economics, but still a model, not a guaranteed P/E |
| Matched term-curve shape | Same-date, comparable-vessel 1/2/3-year quotations and completed fixtures | Helps locate where pricing strength persists; not a probability or a curve made from unrelated deals |

For **cash-cost coverage**, the denominator can include vessel operating costs, corporate cash costs, cash interest, mandatory maintenance and scheduled debt repayments. Do **not** add depreciation to those cash outflows. For **accounting EPS**, use depreciation/interest rather than debt principal or capital expenditure. State the basis before comparing the two.

Unavailable/off-hire days generate neither modeled fixed hire nor assumed spot revenue, while applicable owner costs continue. Use the same available-day population on both sides of the coverage ratio; contractual calendar duration must not be divided by fewer operational days to manufacture coverage above full utilization.

For forward EPS:

```text
FTM earnings scenario
  = contracted net hire within the horizon
  + explicitly assumed net revenue on uncovered days
  - full-company operating, depreciation, finance and tax costs
```

Here **net revenue** is after voyage expenses and applicable commissions, but **before** owner operating costs, G&A, depreciation, interest and tax. Check the actual TCE definition and never deduct its included voyage expenses or commissions again. Convert modeled earnings attributable to common shareholders into **FTM EPS using projected weighted-average shares on a stated basic or diluted basis**, then calculate **FTM P/E = share price / FTM EPS**; do not divide share price by aggregate company profit.

No whole-company number is published here because the complete forward charter book, guaranteed base-rate schedule and company costs have not been reconstructed. A subset of named contracts cannot be silently treated as the entire fleet or as a certified earnings floor.

### An important term-curve trap

For a purely hypothetical **same vessel, same date, same terms**, undiscounted comparison:

```text
1 year at $250,000/day versus 3 years at $100,000/day
Years 2-3 residual average = (3 × $100,000 − $250,000) / 2
                          = $25,000/day
```

This is an arithmetic illustration, **not an inferred forward rate for the actual observed contracts**. Different delivery dates, credit, vessel specifications, trading flexibility, options and discounting invalidate the simple calculation. The useful question is whether pricing strength is extending into longer tenors on comparable terms, not whether two unrelated rates produce an attractive ratio.

### What the historical test does and does not establish

Static/dynamic spreads can flag stale earnings or one-off distortions, but the price cancels from their ratio. Historical high three-year contracts in late 2008 coexisted with a falling equity market; they did not automatically protect the uncontracted fleet or the residual ship value.

A proper signal study would require point-in-time charter books, dated comparable broker rates and fixtures, all costs and total shareholder returns across several cycles. It should test uncovered-day exposure, expiry concentration and renewals out of sample. The present sparse fixture sample cannot establish optimal thresholds or an information lead.

## 6. Valuation discipline

A fixed charter strengthens the explicit cash-flow portion of a valuation. It does not make a one-year windfall permanent. Value the remaining contracted cash flow, then separately model re-chartering and the asset's residual value.

Do not add the entire charter profit stream to a vessel valuation that already includes those attached-contract economics. If using a charter-free ship value, the contract's value is not automatically its entire gross hire; the relevant comparison includes alternative employment, costs, timing and credit.

The practical research priority is **actual base-rate coverage and its expiry profile**, followed by comparable term-market evidence and cost-adjusted forward scenarios. Static, TTM and annualized P/E are cross-checks, not independent votes for a trade. This extension does not validate or trigger the previous staged-exit template.

**Day1Global / pre-mortem:** revenue visibility improves only for covered days; accounting earnings differ from free cash flow; management's coverage choices surrender some upside; one charterer can concentrate credit risk. Failure cases include lower replacement hire, unexpected off-hire, counterparty failure and residual asset repricing. Probabilities, 12-/24-month targets and a verified company-wide profit floor remain **unknown**.

## Step 2 — Strict peer review

### 1. Facts that need verification

No $250,000/day one-year named fixture was established. The broker observation is an archived assessment. Historical fixtures are selective market reports, and 2006 two-year evidence remains missing. Exact base rates, optional periods, commission allocation and all current company charter schedules are incomplete.

### 2. Logical leaps / equivocation

Dynamic P/E is definition-dependent. An assessment is not a signed contract; a contract is not guaranteed net profit; a full charter year is not the next calendar year. An average multi-year rate is not a guaranteed annual minimum. An EPS ratio is not independent price information.

### 3. Missing counterexamples / competing explanations

Contracted ships can support cash generation while uncontracted earnings and ship prices fall. A low annualized-quarter P/E can be caused by exceptional gains; a high one can precede recovery. Rate differences can arise from ship quality, contract flexibility and counterparty credit rather than pure expectations.

### 4. The most important primary sources to add

Complete charterparty economics or owner minimum-revenue schedules, actual commencement/redelivery dates, point-in-time broker curves for matched specifications, and vessel/company costs. These are necessary for a defensible company-level forward EPS anchor.

### 5. Speculation rather than fact

"$80m is guaranteed next year," "the whole fleet can fix at $230,000," "three-year $100,000 predicts years 2-3," and "this P/E spread predicts the top" are unsupported. The profit and term-spread cases are conditional illustrations.
