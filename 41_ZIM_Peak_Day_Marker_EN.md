# 🔎 ZIM's March 2022 Peak — What Actually Happened, and What It Tells Us About the VLCC Top

### Using the container-shipping peak day as a template for predicting the VLCC equity peak

*October 13, 2026 — VLCC Analysis Series #41*

> **Disclaimer**: Research only, not investment advice. Price data: Yahoo Finance daily bars (unadjusted for dividends). Event/rate data: ZIM press release (Mar 9, 2022) and secondary sources (SCFI/WCI levels are approximate — verify before relying on them).

## 0. Premise check (read this first)

The working assumption was *"ZIM peaked on 2022-03-31 after an 8% jump, then fell from ~$100 to ~$6."* The tape says otherwise:

| Assumption | Actual (Yahoo daily data) |
|---|---|
| Peak on 2022-03-31 | **Intraday high $91.23 on 2022-03-17; closing high $88.62 on 2022-03-16.** 3/31 closed $72.71 (−0.2%), ~18% below the peak |
| +8% on the peak day | No +8% day near the peak. Largest up-days: 3/14 +4.7%, 3/16 +4.0%, 3/21 +4.2%. The only ≥7% up-days in H1-2022 were Apr 27 (+7.6%), **May 3 (+8.8%)**, May 13, May 23 — all *lower highs* |
| ~$100 | Never above $91.23 |
| Fell to ~$6 | **Yes: closing low $6.59 on 2023-11-28 (−93%)** |

So the "marker" is not a single 3/31 candle. It is a **six-session topping window (Mar 14–21) ending at the ex-dividend date (Mar 22)**. Whether this was a "blow-off" or dividend-related positioning is an interpretation, not tested here.

## 2. What happened around the peak

1. **Mar 9, 2022 — record Q4/FY2021 results + capital return.** Q4 net income $1.71B ($14.17/sh); FY2021 net income $4.65B ($39.02/sh), adj. EBITDA $6.60B (reported by ZIM as +537% y/y, implying FY20 ≈ $1.04B). The **$17.00/sh** dividend brought the cumulative 2021 payout, including the earlier interim, to 50% of FY2021 net income; **ex-date Mar 22**, record Mar 23, paid Apr 4.
2. **2022 guidance implies decelerating growth.** FY2022 adj. EBITDA guided **$7.1–7.5B vs $6.60B in 2021 (+7.6% to +13.6%)**; adj. EBIT $5.6–6.0B. After a +537% EBITDA year, guided growth fell to single/low-double digits. Whether this was below consensus is **unknown** (not checked).
3. **Timing relative to the ex-date.** The stock rose ~+50% from the Dec-2021 close into Mar 16–21; the $17 payout was ~19% of the price. The closing and intraday highs fell **four and three trading sessions before the ex-date**. Whether dividend-related positioning contributed is **untested**. On Mar 22 the unadjusted close fell 12.2% ($10.73), less than the $17 distribution; including the dividend entitlement, the gross close-to-close return was ≈ +7.1% — the ex-date drop is mechanical, not evidence of selling pressure.
4. **Contemporaneous backdrop (not isolated as causes):** Russia–Ukraine war, Shenzhen lockdown (Mar 14) and Shanghai lockdown (Mar 28), Fed's first hike (Mar 16), and a heavily-shorted stock. This study does **not** isolate the contribution of dividends, freight rates, market conditions or positioning.
5. **Rates were reportedly past peak (secondary, unverified):** Drewry WCI peak **Sep 2021 (~$10.4K/FEU)**; SCFI peak **Jan 2022 (~5,100)**, ~4,500–5,000 in March. In this one case the equity closing high came ~2 months after the SCFI peak and ~6 months after the WCI peak (retrospective observation, not a timing law; the two indices give different lags).

Observation: these three selected stocks' closing highs span ~4 months (Maersk → Hapag). They are correlated exposures to one cycle, not independent replications; this is a descriptive span, not a predictive window. ZIM 6 months after its peak (mid-Sep 2022): $29.53 (−67%).

**Holder's view (dividends matter):** a hypothetical buyer at the $88.62 close on 3/16 who held to 2023-11-28 received $17.00 (Mar 22) + $2.85 + $4.75 + $2.95 + $6.40 (through Apr 2023) = **$33.95/sh**. Dividends + the $6.59 close = $40.54 → a **gross, non-reinvested holding-period return of ≈ −54.3%** (before taxes and costs), versus −93% on price alone. This is not a maximum total-return drawdown. Price-only charts understate the cash returned (relevant to the dividend-cushion framework in Report #19, §9).

Untested research hypotheses (not trading rules; no outcome comparison against alternatives has been done):

- **H1:** Waiting for a large down-day "confirmation" may cost most of the exit price — in ZIM, the first clean breakdown was the ex-date (−12.2% unadjusted, three sessions after the high), and a further −35% followed by Jun-30 ($72.71 on 3/31 → $47.23). One case; no alternative rule tested.
- **H2:** Equity tops may cluster in the months around the rate/earnings peak (Maersk Jan → ZIM Mar → Hapag May). The window length depends on the benchmark chosen (SCFI ≈ 2 months, WCI ≈ 6 months).
- **H3:** For 85–100% payout names, include dividends in return and target math.

## 6. Caveats & open items

1. **One cycle, one stock** — ZIM-specific features (heavily shorted, 50% payout policy) may not generalize. Maersk and Hapag topped on *different* dates, so the "window" is wide.
2. **SCFI/WCI figures** come from secondary sources (peak ≈5,109 SCFI Jan-2022; ≈$10,377 WCI Sep-2021; March level approximate). Re-pull from Shanghai Shipping Exchange / Drewry before back-testing.
3. **VLCC demand is more inelastic** and the orderbook is far smaller; the container *rate collapse* magnitude (SCFI −70% in 12 months) should not be assumed. The more transferable lessons are *timing structure* (rates → guidance → dividend → equity) not magnitude.
4. Prior repo text (Report #19) cites ZIM's peak as "$91.23 (Mar 2022)"; the precise date is **2022-03-17 intraday / 2022-03-16 close ($88.62)**.

**Suggested next step:** back-test flags 1–9 on FRO/DHT/INSW for the 2008, 2015, 2020 and May-2024 tanker tops (FRO peaked 2024-05-28 at $29.21 and fell −54% to Dec-2024; DHT 2024-05-21 $12.71 → −30%; INSW 2024-05-24 $65.13 → −50% — all closes), and extend `modeling_stash.md` with the ex-dividend anchor.
