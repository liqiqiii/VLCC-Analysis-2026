## §1 — Method

The question is: **is "follow Zijin" a strategy, or a story?**

The design deliberately removes the two ways this kind of study usually lies:

1. **Anchor on the PUBLIC ANNOUNCEMENT DATE, never the low.** Every return below is what an outsider could have earned buying at the close *after* the news, or at the disclosed subscription price. This is why §4 rejects the "2020 low" framing even though it flatters the thesis.
2. **Separate what is followable from what is not.** In a **full takeover** the target delists and an outside holder is cashed out at the premium once — none of the subsequent value creation accrues to them. Those deals are evidence about Zijin, not a strategy. Only **minority stakes and JVs where the partner stayed listed** are backtested.

Categories used throughout:

| Category | Meaning | Can an outsider follow? |
|---|---|---|
| **FOLLOWABLE** | Minority stake / project JV; target stayed listed | ✅ Yes |
| **DEEPENING** | Zijin raised an existing stake or took control, target still listed | ✅ Yes |
| **TAKEOVER** | 100% acquisition; target delisted | ❌ No — cashed out once |
| **SPINOFF** | Zijin carve-out (Zijin Gold International) | ✅ but it is Zijin, not a partner |

Benchmarks are **Zijin's own A-shares** (the honest hurdle — why not just buy Zijin?), plus **copper and gold** (the commodity-beta hurdle that §6 shows is the real challenge to the thesis).

## §2 — Step 1: Concise Research Draft

### Core conclusion

**Co-investing alongside Zijin in a shared asset has paid extraordinarily well — median 7.6x across seven seasoned situations, beating Zijin itself in five of seven. But the causal mechanism is only partly "Zijin," a large share is commodity beta and small-cap concentration, the value is realised at ASSET COMMISSIONING rather than at announcement, and the newest deals carry a new and unhedgeable risk: Chinese regulatory approval of Zijin's own outbound investment.**

### Supporting point 1

**Claim → the listed minority partner systematically out-returns the operator, because the shared asset dominates its NAV.**
*Evidence needed:* partner vs Zijin returns from identical announcement dates. **Obtained — §5.** Zangge **1.67x** Zijin from the Julong deal; Ivanhoe **1.10x**; Montage **5.68x**; Wanguo **3.65x**.

### Supporting point 2

**Claim → the pattern is repeatable, not a single lucky case.**
*Evidence needed:* more than one instance of the identical structure. **Obtained — §5, §7.** Zijin has done it at least three times: **Julong/Zangge (2020)**, **Kamoa/Ivanhoe (2015)**, **Haiyu/Zhaojin (2022)** — in each case a **project-level stake plus a listco stake** in the same asset.

### Supporting point 3

**Claim → Zijin buys stalled or distressed assets it can fix, so the value-add is operational and therefore real.**
*Evidence needed:* the condition of the assets at acquisition. **Obtained — §7.** Julong was **halted in H2-2019** on the prior owner's funding shortfall; Chifeng had just **missed 2025 production** on Wassa grade decline; Nevsun's Timok was stuck; Continental Gold had security problems.

### Opposing point 1 (against my own conclusion)

**Claim → most of the return is commodity beta, not Zijin.**
*Evidence needed:* a case where Zijin is present but does NOT operate. **Obtained, and it is damaging — §6.** **Western Mining rose ~6.5x with Zijin as a passive ~22% non-operating minority in Yulong copper.** Further, the roster's biggest winners are **gold** names measured through a historic gold bull market, and gold rose from ~US$1,900 to **US$4,427**.

### Opposing point 2 (against my own conclusion)

**Claim → the structure alone is not sufficient; Zhaojin proves it.**
*Evidence needed:* an instance of the identical structure that underperformed. **Obtained — §7.2.** **Zhaojin returned 3.00x = 0.73x Zijin**, despite having the same project-stake-plus-listco-stake architecture. The distinguishing variable is that **Haiyu has not commissioned while Julong Phase II has.**

## §6 — ⚠️ The attribution problem: is this Zijin, or is it just copper and gold?

**This section exists because the peer review demanded it, and it is the most serious challenge to the thesis.**

### 6.1 The near-control experiment

**西部矿业 Western Mining (601168.SS)** holds **Yulong copper** (玉龙铜矿 — a *different* mine from Julong 巨龙; the near-homophone is a real trap). Zijin holds a **~22% minority that does NOT operate the mine.** Western Mining nonetheless rose roughly **6.5x**.

> **If a passive, non-operating Zijin minority coincides with 6.5x, then "Zijin's operatorship creates the return" is not established.** The most parsimonious explanation for much of this roster is: **these are small, asset-concentrated equities in commodities that went up a lot.**

### 6.2 The benchmark that matters

Over the study window, **gold went from roughly US$1,900 to US$4,427/oz** and copper roughly doubled. The roster's two standouts — **Montage (11.38x)** and **Wanguo (7.62x)** — are **gold** names, and Montage is a **pre-production developer with no earnings** (P/B 13.8, negative ROE): a long-duration call option on the gold price. **A rising gold price alone would produce spectacular returns in such a security whether or not Zijin were involved.**

### 6.3 What survives the challenge

Three things still argue for a genuine, if smaller, Zijin effect:

1. **The stall-and-fix record is specific and verifiable.** Julong was **halted in H2-2019** and is now commissioning a **350,000 t/day** Phase II. Zangge could not do that; Zijin could.
2. **The partner beats the operator, which pure beta does not explain.** If it were only commodity beta, Zangge and Zijin — both copper-levered — should move together. Zangge beat Zijin **1.67x**. NAV concentration is a real, mechanical edge.
3. **Zijin's entry is often at a discount** (Wanguo −9.95% to the reference close; Chifeng's H tranche below the 60-day average), which is evidence of **negotiating power**, though the outsider never receives that discount.

### 6.4 The honest formulation

> **"Follow Zijin" is best understood as a SCREEN, not an alpha engine.** It reliably surfaces **small, asset-concentrated, high-torque miners in commodities a sophisticated strategic buyer is committing capital to.** Whether the subsequent return is Zijin's doing or the commodity's **cannot be separated with n=7 in a decade-long bull market.** The strategy's dependence on the commodity cycle is therefore **much higher than the narrative suggests** — and in a falling gold or copper market, the same NAV concentration that produced 15x will work in reverse (see the drawdowns in §9.3).

## §13 — Method for Part II, and the one error I had to fix first

### 13.1 Three independent valuation methods

For a gold miner, any single method lies. Part II uses three:

| # | Method | What it catches | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Earnings bridge** — gold price → cash margin → net income → P/E | The honest core; built from each company's OWN output and cost base | Ignores by-products, assumes a flat tax rate |
| **2** | **EV per ounce** — EV ÷ annual production, EV ÷ reserve ounce | When an earnings model is flattered by a temporary gold price | Needs reserve disclosure (Chifeng has none) |
| **3** | **FCF yield** — Day1Global Module C, "THE BIG ONE" | A miner that cannot convert profit into cash | Distorted in a heavy build-out year |

### 13.2 🔴 The error I made, and the fix

My first run produced **Allied Gold +697% in the bull case**. That is nonsense, and the cause is worth stating because it is the same error caught in this repo's VLCC capstone:

> **Applying a BULL multiple to BULL earnings counts the same optimism twice.**

Cyclical miners trade at **high P/Es on depressed earnings and LOW P/Es on peak earnings** (CRule 2). So the corrected scenarios below **cut the multiple as gold rises**: **12x conservative → 10x base → 7x bull.**

### 13.3 🔴 The second fix: calibrating to reality

A valuation model never back-tested against reported results is a random number generator. Gold averaged **~US$3,050/oz in 2025**, so the bridge should reproduce FY2025:

| Company | Modelled FY2025 net | **ACTUAL FY2025 net** | Error | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 赤峰黄金 Chifeng | US$437m | **US$460m** | **−5%** | ✅ Good fit — its cost assumption is trustworthy |
| 招金矿业 Zhaojin | US$644m | **US$540m** | +19% | ✅ Acceptable (model ignores low-margin smelting) |
| **Allied Gold** | **US$270m** | **🔴 −US$63m (a LOSS)** | **n/m** | **🔴 POOR FIT — a US$333m gap** |

**Allied clears its AISC and still loses money.** That US$333m gap is D&A, interest and growth capex that **AISC does not capture**. Every scenario below therefore **subtracts each company's own validation gap** (Chifeng −US$23m, Zhaojin +US$105m, Allied +US$333m). Without this calibration Allied's targets are fantasy.

> **This is the single most important methodological point in Part II: AISC is not profit, and for a high-cost, high-D&A miner the difference is the whole investment case.**
