## Step 1 — Research draft

**Core conclusion.**

- **Several options remain, but their ability to produce a ≥10¢ national pump-price reduction before 3-Nov is unestablished.**
  - Most quick tools are already in use (§1); delivered volumes for several are unknown.
  - A Hormuz navigation arrangement that lifts the blockade is the option most likely to move prices materially, and it has so far been rejected.
- **The remaining Hormuz export shortfall is concentrated in refined products, not crude.** Kpler, via CNBC on 30-Sep [P]:
  - Crude through Hormuz was at its pre-war baseline (13.5 mb/d, 7-day average).
  - Products through Hormuz were 0.68 mb/d vs 3.6 mb/d pre-war.
  - How much of US pump prices this explains, relative to crude costs, is **not established** here.
- **For tankers, the evidence cannot yet distinguish a temporary equity correction from freight normalization.**
  - Crude *throughput* has recovered.
  - But *effective tanker capacity* is unverified: vessel-days per barrel are raised by ship-to-ship transfers, rerouting, waiting and ballasting.
  - Today, the BWET ETF share price fell ~9.6% intraday; an equivalent fall in the underlying freight futures is **unverified**.
  - So both "short-term noise" (the user's view) and "rates rolling over, as with ZIM" (Report #41) remain **hypotheses**.

**Supporting points (claim → evidence needed)**

1. *Most fast levers are already used* → policy record. **Have it [S]**:
   - SPR at 283 mb after a 172 mb release (delivered amount unverified); a 40 mb loan is only a solicitation.
   - IEA: 400 mb in March; G7 100 mb on 2-Oct, which the EU says was the March remainder.
   - The US is lobbying for ≥100 mb more (Bloomberg via World Oil, 15-Oct [P]).
   - Jones Act waivers.
   - Diesel export ban ruled out.
   - Gas-tax holiday and E15 stalled.
   - Russian diesel GL 135.
2. *The remaining physical shortfall is concentrated in products* → Hormuz flow data by product. **Have it [P]** (Kpler via CNBC, 30-Sep). It is late-September data and may have changed by 15-Oct.
3. *The Russian diesel deal is small and mostly later-dated* → tranche schedule. **Have it [S]**:
   - Immediate tranche 0.3 Mt ≈ 2.2 mb.
   - Firm total 1.8 Mt ≈ 13.4 mb, about 150–220 kb/d if spread over 60–90 days [E].
   - Delivery performance, and which constraint binds (ships, insurance, banking, refinery output), are **not established**.

**Opposing points (claim → evidence needed)**

1. *Tanker scarcity persists despite normal crude throughput* → fleet utilization, voyage durations, TD3C and FFA prints after 9-Oct. **Unknown.** Ship-to-ship transfers ("most crude crossing the strait changes tankers offshore", Kpler [P]) indicate continuing logistical friction.
2. *A Hormuz navigation arrangement could happen without ending the war* → a deal text. **Unknown.** Iran calls the US reply "insufficient" [S]. A WSJ report via CNBC [S] says Trump expects renewed strikes after the vote.
