## Step 1 — Research draft

**Core conclusion.**

- **The shadow fleet is not one fleet:**
  - The ships **loading at Russian ports** are mainly mid-size crude carriers (Aframax/Suezmax) and small product tankers.
  - The Iranian and ex-Venezuelan trades used many VLCCs.
- **No formal shadow-fleet easing was found for 14-Oct:**
  - Iran was **tightened**: 17 tankers designated on 8-Oct, under a naval blockade.
  - Russia's 9-Oct waiver (OFAC GL 135) is reported to cover **diesel**; its text was not verified here.
- **The evidence weakens a narrow diesel/product-led explanation for today's selloff, but does not identify the main driver.** Product tankers fell less than their beta to crude tankers implied. But Suezmax-focused NAT fell the most (−6.6%), so broader Russia-relief *expectations* cannot be excluded.
- **Normalization of Iranian trade is a potentially material VLCC downside scenario.** Its size is unquantified. Released ships come back together with restored cargo, and age/compliance screens constrain and may delay re-entry.

## 4. Would sanctions relief hurt VLCC owners? Mechanisms (scenarios, not forecasts)

Four distinct levers need separate treatment:

1. **Blockade / Hormuz access.** Releases trapped compliant tonnage and laden Iranian tankers.
2. **Cargo-sanctions relief.** Makes Iranian or Russian barrels legally tradeable.
3. **Vessel delisting.** Removes ship-level designations. This does **not** follow automatically from cargo relief, and US, UK and EU lists differ.
4. **Trade-pattern change.** Restored Gulf barrels may displace longer-haul imports, cutting tonne-miles; they may also add net cargo.

Key considerations:

- **Age and compliance.** In the May-2026 KSE sample, more than 90% of Russian-loading shadow tankers were older than 15 years [P]. Charterer vetting weighs age together with condition, class, insurance and ownership. It is a screening factor, not a ban. Age and compliance requirements **constrain and may delay re-entry**. **The size of the returning fleet that could actually compete is unquantified.** Returning ships can also affect mainstream owners indirectly, by substitution, without entering oil-major trades.
- **Efficiency release.** Sanctioned trade uses ship-to-ship transfers, dark routing and floating storage. Normalization could raise *effective* supply (repo, vlcc_seasonality §4). This has not been quantified here.
- **Cargo offset (sign).** Restored *net incremental* tonne-miles support tanker demand and offset released capacity. UANI's August-2026 tracker [P] states that "pre-war monthly figures typically averaged around 55 million barrels per month" (~1.8 mb/d). The tracker does not define "pre-war" or the averaging period. But gross Iranian barrels may displace other suppliers or shorten voyages, and they may be carried by the same shadow ships that are "released".
- **By country (directional only):**
  - Russian relief would bear mostly on Aframax/Suezmax and product segments.
  - Iranian normalization is the scenario most directly tied to VLCCs.
  - Neither magnitude is established.
